How to Search the 23,000+ PYQ Archive Like a Topper

23,000+ Delhi University papers in one search box. Here is how to pull your exact paper in seconds instead of scrolling through hundreds of collections.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 23 September 2026

The PYQ Archive search holds every Delhi University previous year paper on Sulaksh — 23,000+ papers across Honours, Programme, GE, SEC, VAC and AEC courses. Most students type one word, skim, and give up. Toppers search differently. This guide shows the exact method.

Step 1: Start from your paper code, not the subject

Paper names repeat across courses — "Financial Accounting" exists in B.Com (Hons), B.Com (Prog) and as a GE paper, each with different units. Before searching, copy the paper code (DSC-3, GE-2, SEC-4…) from your syllabus or admit card. Search the paper name first, then use the code shown on each result to confirm it is yours.

Example: searching corporate accounting returns DSC papers from two programmes. The result line shows semester and year — match those against your date sheet before downloading.

Step 2: Type the exact paper name

The search matches paper titles, so precision beats length:

  • Good: data privacy → every Data Privacy PYQ across years and sets, one list.
  • Good: cost accounting → Cost Accounting papers only, newest first.
  • Weak: accounting → hundreds of hits across Financial, Cost, Corporate and Management Accounting. You will scroll instead of revise.
  • Weak: sem 5 bcom question paper → too many common words; the engine matches words, not meaning.

Rule of thumb: 2–4 distinctive words — the paper's actual name as printed on the question paper.

Step 3: Read the result line before opening

Every result shows three things after the title: subject · semester · year. This is your verification strip. A "Business Laws 2024 Sem 1" result and a "Business Laws 2026 Sem 1" result can test different units if the syllabus was revised — always open the one matching your semester and the UGCF pattern (2023 onwards).

Sets matter: titles ending in Set 1 / Set 2 / (2) are parallel sets of the same session — same syllabus, different questions. Solving two sets of one session beats solving one set each of two distant years.

Step 4: Fewer words when you get zero hits

No matches usually means over-specifying, not a missing paper. Shorten step by step:

  1. Drop the session first: data privacy nov dec 2024 → data privacy.
  2. Drop filler words: introduction to western philosophy → western philosophy.
  3. Try the core noun alone: epistemology, jurisprudence, macroeconomics.
  4. Check spelling variants: organisation vs organization — DU papers use both across years.

If a single distinctive word still returns nothing, the paper may be listed under its course name (e.g. GE papers under the offering department). Browse the subject collections instead, or ask on the contact page — missing-paper requests are uploaded within 6–10 hours.

Step 5: View vs Save — pick the right one

Each result has two buttons. View opens the paper in the browser reader — best on the phone, no download, never forced. Save downloads the PDF for offline revision and printing. For exam-week revision, save the 3–4 papers you will actually solve and view the rest.

Step 6: Combine search with the 3-pass method

Searching finds papers; the 3-pass PYQ method converts them into marks. Pull 3–4 recent papers for your subject with the search above, then: Pass 1 — map every question to syllabus Units 1–4; Pass 2 — solve one full paper timed (3 hours, no phone); Pass 3 — compress each unit to a one-page sheet. The search box gets you the raw material in seconds; the method turns it into a prediction tool.

When browsing beats searching

Search is a sniper; sometimes you need a map. Use the subject collections instead when:

  • You don't know the paper name yet. New semester, new subjects — scroll your course hub (e.g. B.Com Programme, Philosophy Honours) to see every paper at a glance, then search the ones you will actually solve.
  • You take GE, SEC, VAC or AEC papers. These live in shared pools across departments with unfamiliar names. Browsing the pool shows you what exists; searching works once you know the exact title.
  • You want everything for one semester. Open your semester folder on the DU & College page — syllabus, notes and PYQs sit side by side in taught order, which no search result can replicate.

Rule of thumb: browse to discover, search to retrieve. First week of the semester — browse. Exam fortnight — search.

A 7-day PYQ sprint using the archive

Here is the routine this search box was built for. One subject, seven days:

  1. Day 1 — pull: search each paper name, save 3 recent PYQs per paper (2023 onwards). Check every result's semester and year line before saving.
  2. Days 2–3 — map: mark every question to syllabus Units 1–4. Rank units by frequency. This tells you where 60% of marks hide.
  3. Days 4–5 — solve timed: one full paper per day, 3 hours, no phone. Mark yourself strictly — DU checking rewards structure (headings, one example per answer, concluding line).
  4. Day 6 — compress: one page per unit — 4–5 bullets, one diagram or table, one PYQ pointer. This sheet is your entire revision.
  5. Day 7 — simulate: one more full paper, morning slot if your exam is in the morning. Then stop. Sleep beats cramming on the last night.

Repeat per subject. Students who run this sprint rarely need anything beyond the archive, their syllabus folder and one standard textbook per paper.

Quick checklist

  • Paper code copied from syllabus or admit card before searching
  • 2–4 distinctive words in the search box, exact paper name
  • Semester + year on the result line matched to my date sheet
  • UGCF pattern only (2023 onwards) for pattern study; older papers for practice volume
  • View on phone, save the 3–4 papers I will actually solve

FAQ

Does the archive cover my course? 23,000+ papers across DU Honours, Programme, GE, SEC, VAC and AEC — if DU set the paper, it is almost certainly there. Use Step 4 if your first query misses.

Are these the official DU papers? Yes — previous year question papers as set by the University of Delhi, free to view and save. Always verify the paper code against your college handout for your batch.

Can I download for offline study? Yes — hit Save on any result. Print 2–3 papers per subject; solving on paper under timed conditions beats scrolling PDFs.
