One-Day Exams: Your First 90 Days

SSC, banking or railways — here is the exact sequence for your first three months: base first, sectionals second, full mocks third, and the traps to ignore completely.

← All Guides

Every year, lakhs of students decide to prepare for government exams — SSC CGL, CHSL, banking, railways — and most quit within three months. Not because the exams are impossible, but because they start without a sequence: they buy six books, join four Telegram groups, attempt a mock on day one, score badly, and vanish. This roadmap prevents exactly that.

Month 1: Build the base, not the speed

Your first month has one goal — make the syllabus boring and familiar.

  • Quantitative Aptitude: start with arithmetic chapters that every exam shares — percentages, ratio, average, profit & loss, time & work, speed-distance. Learn the concept, then solve 20–30 basic questions per chapter. Speed comes later; accuracy of method comes now.
  • Reasoning: coding-decoding, series, blood relations, directions, syllogism. These are pattern libraries, not maths. Daily 30 minutes is enough in month one.
  • English or Hindi (per your exam): 15 new words a day plus grammar rules one topic at a time. Vocabulary compounds — starting early is the whole trick.
  • General awareness: do NOT touch current affairs daily digests yet. Read last 6 months of static GK themes relevant to your exam first.

No mocks this month. Mocks before basics measure nothing and demoralise everyone.

Month 2: Topic practice + first sectional tests

Now add timed practice. Finish remaining arithmetic and reasoning topics, but shift the ratio: 40% learning, 60% solving. Take sectional tests (one section at a time) once a week. Maintain an error notebook from day one of testing — every wrong answer gets one line explaining its cause.

Start one daily current-affairs source now, and cap it at 30–45 minutes. More than that is procrastination dressed as dedication.

Month 3: Full mocks + analysis loops

Two full-length mocks per week, reviewed the same day. The review matters more than the mock: reclassify every error into concept gap / calculation slip / question selection mistake. Your scores will jump not from studying more chapters but from killing repeat errors.

  • Fix question-selection strategy: in most exams, attempting 80% accurately beats attempting 100% with panic.
  • Set per-section time budgets and practise abandoning questions that cross them.
The golden rule: prepare for ONE exam family at a time. SSC and banking overlap heavily, so pick your primary target by syllabus fit and let secondary exams ride on the same preparation — but never study two unrelated patterns simultaneously as a beginner.

What to ignore completely

  • "Topper strategy" videos that assume 10 free hours a day.
  • Paid test series in month one — free sectional tests are enough until you finish basics.
  • New Telegram/PDF groups weekly. Material-hoarding feels like work and isn't.

Ninety days done honestly puts you ahead of the majority who are still collecting resources. Keep your material in one place — the ONE DAY EXAMS section on Sulaksh organises notes, PYQs and question banks so you spend evenings practising instead of hunting.

FAQ

Can I prepare for SSC and banking together? Their quant and reasoning sections overlap heavily, so one preparation serves both. But pick a primary exam early because English depth and general awareness patterns differ.

Is coaching necessary for government exams? No. Disciplined self-study with structured material and regular mocks covers the syllabus. Coaching mainly buys accountability and pace.

How many mocks before the real exam? Roughly 30–40 full mocks with same-day analysis across your preparation, spaced increasingly closer in the final two months. Quality of review matters more than count.
