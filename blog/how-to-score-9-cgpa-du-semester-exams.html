How to Score 9+ CGPA in DU Semester Exams

A 9+ CGPA at DU means averaging an A+ grade across your papers — roughly 80%+ in most papers. Here's the playbook.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 21 September 2026

A 9+ CGPA at DU means averaging an A+ grade (9 grade points) across your papers — roughly 80%+ in most papers. That's ambitious but entirely doable, because DU's marking system has predictable, bankable chunks that most students leave on the table. Here's the playbook.

First, understand the maths

DU uses a 10-point grading system:

GradePointsRough marks range
O (Outstanding)1090–100%
A+ (Excellent)980–89%
A (Very Good)870–79%
B+ (Good)760–69%
B (Above Average)655–59%
C (Average)550–54%
P (Pass)440–49%
F (Fail)0Below 40%

Your SGPA each semester is Σ (grade points × credits) ÷ Σ credits. Two things follow from this formula:

  1. Credits are weights. A 4-credit DSC paper moves your SGPA twice as much as a 2-credit SEC paper. An A+ in a 4-credit paper is worth more than an O in a 2-credit one.
  2. Consistency beats spikes. One O can't rescue three B's. Aim for A/A+ across the board rather than perfection in one paper.

(For reference, DU marksheets convert CGPA to percentage as CGPA × 9.5.)

Step 1: Bank the 25% internal assessment

Every theory paper reserves 25 marks for internal assessment — and this is the easiest scoring in your degree:

  • Attendance: 5 marks. 85%+ attendance gets full marks. Just show up.
  • Assignments: 10 marks. Submit everything, on time, with genuine effort. Neat, complete assignments rarely score badly.
  • Class tests: 10 marks. Treat every class test like a mini end-semester exam.

Walk into the end-semester exam with 20+/25 in internals and you need roughly 60/75 in the final paper for an A+. Walk in with 12/25 and you need near-perfection. The semester is won or lost before the exam fortnight.

Step 2: Treat SEC, VAC and AEC as free marks

Your 2-credit SEC, VAC and AEC papers have compact syllabi — that's not a rumour, it's structural. Less syllabus per paper means each hour of study covers more of the exam. These are the papers where an O (10 points) is genuinely within reach, and they quietly lift your whole SGPA. Never "wing" them.

Step 3: Weight your effort by credits

With limited days before exams, study time should roughly follow credit weight:

  • 4-credit DSC papers first — these decide your SGPA. Deep preparation here.
  • Then GE and DSE papers — also 4 credits, same logic.
  • Then the 2-credit papers — compact enough to finish fast (see Step 2).

If you're short on time, an extra day on a 4-credit paper beats an extra day polishing a 2-credit one.

Step 4: Use PYQs with the 3-pass method

Previous year papers are the single highest-ROI revision tool at DU — concepts repeat across years. But don't just read them. Use the 3-pass method:

  • Pass 1 — Map: Go through 3–4 recent papers and mark which syllabus unit each question came from. You'll immediately see which units DU actually asks about (it's never evenly spread).
  • Pass 2 — Solve timed: Pick one full paper and solve it in 3 hours, exam conditions, no phone. This is where you discover you "know" things you can't actually write in time.
  • Pass 3 — Compress: Make a one-page sheet per unit — one quote, diagram or formula per unit. This compresses ~200 pages of notes into ~20 revision pages for the night before the exam.

Stick to 2023–2025 papers — these follow the current UGCF pattern. Older CBCS-era papers are fine for background but can test different units, so always cross-check the paper code against your current syllabus.

Grab your course's papers on Sulaksh, where PYQs are organised by course, semester and paper — for instance, the B.Com Programme PYQ hub has semester-wise papers ready to download.

Step 5: Follow a revision timeline

  • 6 weeks out: Finish the syllabus once. Start Pass 1 of PYQs alongside.
  • 2 weeks out: Pass 2 — timed papers, one per subject. Fix weak units.
  • Last 3 days: Only your one-page sheets (Pass 3) and short notes. No new topics — new material now just creates panic.
  • Night before: Sleep. A rested brain recalls more than a crammed one.

Step 6: Write for the examiner

DU papers are checked fast, in bulk. Help your examiner help you:

  • Answer the question asked, not the question you prepared for.
  • Use headings, sub-headings and bullet points — walls of text get skimmed.
  • Diagrams, flowcharts and examples earn marks disproportionately to the effort they take.
  • Attempt every question. There's no negative marking — a structured attempt at a question you half-know beats a blank.

The mistakes that cost toppers their 9

  • Ignoring internals until it's too late ("I'll cover it in the end-sem" — you can't; those marks are gone).
  • Studying from a senior's CBCS-era notes without checking the current syllabus.
  • Spending equal time on every paper regardless of credits.
  • Reading PYQs like a newspaper instead of solving them timed.
  • Starting revision with the toughest unit instead of the highest-weightage one (check Pass 1 first).

A 9+ CGPA isn't about studying 12 hours a day. It's about banking the predictable marks, weighting effort by credits, and revising from the papers DU actually sets. Start with your internals this week — future-you will be grateful.
