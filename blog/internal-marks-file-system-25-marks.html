DU Internal Marks: The 25-Mark File System

25 marks per paper, decided before exam fortnight. I left 8 of them on the table in Sem 1 — here's the system that banks them.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the cheapest marks of your degree — the ones most students donate to the university for free.

In Sem 1, I walked into every theory paper needing 65+ out of 75 for an A. Why? Because my internals averaged 13 out of 25. I'd skipped classes in September ("attendance ka kya hai"), submitted one assignment late with coffee stains on it, and treated the class test like a surprise I was supposed to fail. My friend with the exact same exam-hall answers as me got an A+ in two papers where I got an A. Same knowledge. Twelve marks of file-and-attendance difference. That's when it clicked: the semester is won or lost before exam fortnight.

Here's the rub: every DU theory paper reserves 25 marks for internal assessment, and students treat them as guaranteed pocket money while doing nothing to earn them. Regular-college students bleed attendance marks to society fests and long weekends; SOL students miss assignment deadlines because nobody reminds them twice. Then everyone tries to recover those 10 lost marks in the 75-mark theory paper — where each mark costs five times the effort.

From navigating this university business for three years now, I can tell you — 20+/25 in internals is a system, not luck. A file with an index page, dated entries, and one practical per unit, submitted on time, gets full marks in most colleges almost by default. Teachers check 60 files in an evening; make yours the easy full-marks decision. Let's build the system.

The hard truth about internals

Treating internals as "free marks" while ignoring them is the most expensive mistake in DU. The maths is brutal and simple. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • One internal mark = five theory marks of effort. Walking in with 22/25 means needing ~58/75 for an A+. Walking in with 13/25 means needing ~67/75 — near-perfection. Nobody covers a 9-mark internal gap in the exam hall. Ever.
  • Teachers grade files in bulk, fast. Your file is one of 60 checked in an evening. A complete file — index, page numbers, dated entries, neat tables — signals "full marks" in 30 seconds. A stapled pile of undated printouts signals "skim and deduct". Format decides before content is read.
  • 40% theory is separate — internals can't rescue a blank sheet. A 24/25 internal with 25/75 theory is still an ER in most schemes. Internals lower the bar you must clear; they don't remove it. Check your college handout for the exact split — most run attendance 5 + assignment 10 + class test 10.

SOL students note: your internal route runs through assignments submitted by deadline (check your SOL dashboard dates, not rumours). A missed assignment deadline is a zero no theory paper can fix. Find your paper codes on your fee receipt and match every submission to the right UPC — wrong-code submissions are the silent killer.

The file system (build once, reuse every paper)

Week 1 of semester: set up the skeleton. One file per practical/file paper. First three pages, always: index page (experiment number, title, date, page), certificate/declaration if your college wants it, then entries begin. Number every page now — page numbers added at the end always go wrong. This 30-minute setup is what separates full-marks files from deduction magnets.

  • Step 1: Index page → experiment number, title, date, page number. Update it the same day you add an entry.
  • Step 2: One practical per unit, minimum — a Drive folder + Form for Digital Empowerment, a calendar + creatives for Social Media Marketing, a workbook with 3 sheets for Spreadsheets. The file is your revision (see the SEC SMM file guide and Spreadsheets practical guide).
  • Step 3: Every entry gets aim → steps/theory → output/screenshot → 3-line conclusion. Same skeleton everywhere; teachers recognise it instantly.

Mid-sem: the class-test protocol. When a class test is announced, do one timed sitting the night before from a PYQ on Sulaksh PYQs — same 3-pass logic as end-sems, just smaller (read the 3-pass method). In the test, use the exam-hall format: headings, one visual, attempt everything. A 10-mark class test written like an end-sem answer routinely scores 8+; the same content as a paragraph block scores 5.

Last month: submit early, sleep easy. Finish files 10 days before submission week. Teachers remember early, complete files when grading — and you'll walk into exam fortnight with 20+/25 already banked, needing 60 instead of 67 in theory. That gap is the whole game (see the 9+ CGPA playbook and the IA breakup guide).

File secret I learnt late: Dated entries beat undated brilliance. A file where every experiment carries its date looks supervised and genuine — teachers full-mark it without reading closely. And for SOL assignments: submit 2–3 days before the portal deadline, screenshot the confirmation, and keep the PDF. Portal glitches on deadline day have eaten more marks than tough papers.

The 4 internal-mark mistakes that cost SGPA

  • Skipping September classes. Attendance compounds — the 5 marks are gone by October and no amount of December studying buys them back.
  • Late, stained assignments. On-time + neat beats brilliant + late. Teachers deduct first for lateness, then for content.
  • Undated file dumps. Ten printouts stapled the night before submission scream panic. Dated, indexed entries whisper full marks.
  • Ignoring class tests. A 10-mark test treated as a joke is 10 marks treated as a joke. One timed PYQ the night before turns it into 8+.

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: Are internals really that important if I'm good at theory?
Yes — the maths doesn't care how good you are. 22/25 internals means needing ~58/75 for an A+; 13/25 means needing ~67/75. Same brain, different semester outcome. Bank the easy marks first.

Q2: My attendance is already low — is it over?
Not over, just narrower. Maximise what's left: perfect attendance from today, flawless files, and 8+ in every remaining class test. You can still reach 18–20/25, which keeps A+ in play with strong theory.

Q3: SOL student — how do my internals work without regular classes?
Through assignments submitted on your SOL dashboard by the posted deadline. Check dates weekly (not rumours), submit 2–3 days early, screenshot confirmations, and keep PDFs. Missed deadline = zero that theory can't fix.

Q4: How neat does the file really need to be?
Index + page numbers + dates + one visual per entry. That's it — 30 minutes of setup per file. Teachers full-mark complete, dated files in bulk checking; content just needs to be correct, not beautiful.

Q5: Can high internals save weak theory?
They lower the bar, not remove it — you still need 40% in theory separately in most schemes. But they turn "need 67" into "need 58", which is the difference between panic and control in the exam hall.

Last-minute advice from the author

I know internals feel like background noise next to the "real" syllabus. They're not — they're a quarter of every paper, decided while you're still attending classes. Set up your file skeletons this week, fix attendance from tomorrow, and take the next class test like an end-sem.
