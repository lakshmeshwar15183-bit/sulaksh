Marked Absent Though You Gave the Exam? Fix It

A friend wrote her full GE paper and got "Absent" on the marksheet. Three months of follow-ups fixed it — here's the exact route.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the most unfair problem in DU — and the fix nobody explains in one place.

A friend wrote her entire GE paper last semester — three hours, full sheets, signature on the attendance register, everything. Result day: "Absent" next to that subject, plus an ER stapled to it for good measure. She went through every stage of grief in one evening, convinced her job applications were dead. What followed was three months of the least glamorous work in student life: a letter to the exam centre's college for the attendance-sheet copy, a written application with ID and admit card, repeated visits to the examination branch, and calm, relentless follow-ups. The marksheet got corrected. The job process resumed. The whole episode taught our circle something valuable: result errors are administrative problems, and administrative problems yield to paper trails plus persistence — never to panic, and never to waiting quietly.

Here's the rub: this happens every result season, to regular-college students and distance learners alike. Answer sheets get swapped by mistake (one invigilator handed a student the absentee ahead's copy and said "it doesn't matter" — it did), attendance data gets misfed, and the marksheet prints Absent for an exam you visibly sat. The forums fill with identical stories each cycle, and the advice is always fragmented across comments. This post puts the complete route in one place, for both study modes.

From watching two such cases to resolution (one regular, one distance), here is what "Absent" technically means, the evidence that unlocks correction, the exact offices in each route, realistic timelines, and the backup plan if the clock runs out.

The hard truth: this is a paperwork battle, not an academic one

Your knowledge is irrelevant to this problem — only records matter. Internalise that and you'll stop wasting energy in the wrong places. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • Your roll number isn't tied to the sheet number. Anyone can physically write in any answer book — what connects you to your marks is the exam-day attendance record (signature + sheet number logged against your roll number). That's why the single most important document is the attendance-sheet copy from the college where your centre fell. Everything else supports it.
  • DU admin delays everything by default. Ask anyone: the smallest correction takes weeks, sometimes months, and files move only under pressure. "Applied and waiting" for three months with zero follow-ups is how cases die. Plan for fortnightly in-person follow-ups from day one — polite, documented, relentless.
  • An uncorrected Absent becomes an ER. While "Absent" sits on the marksheet, the paper counts as not-cleared — same family as an Essential Repeat (see the ER rules post). So this fix has a deadline shaped like the next exam cycle: resolve it, or be ready to reappear. Both tracks run in parallel below.

The fix, step by step (do all six, in order)

StepWhat to doWhy it matters
1. Freeze the evidenceScreenshot/download the marksheet; note subject, date, centre, and your seat/attendance-sheet number if you remember itWebsites update and memories blur — day-one records anchor everything after
2. Get the attendance-sheet proofVisit the college where your exam centre fell; request a signed copy/printout of that day's attendance sheet showing your signatureThis single page is 80% of your case — it proves presence officially
3. Write the applicationFormal letter: roll number, UPC/paper code, exam date + session, centre, what the marksheet shows vs what happened; attach ID, admit card, fee receipt, attendance proofWritten requests create file movement; phone calls create nothing
4. File on your routeRegular: college exam office → DU examination branch. Distance: learner-support office / dashboard grievance + in-person visit with the same fileEach route has its own desk — filing on the wrong one loses weeks
5. Follow up in person, fortnightlyVisit with your file copy; note names, dates, and assurances; escalate politely but repeatedlyFiles move under pressure — "applied and waiting" quietly is how cases stall for months
6. Insure with the reappear formIf the next cycle's form window opens before correction, file to reappear in that paper as backupA backup form costs a fee; a missed cycle costs six months

On routes: regular-college students start at their own college's exam office (which forwards to the examination branch), while distance learners work through the learner-support office and dashboard grievance channels plus in-person visits — same evidence, same persistence, different desks. Don't let anyone tell you one mode "can't" fix this; both cases I watched got corrected, one from each mode. If your card itself had issues on the day, the admit-card fixes post covers that half of the story.

Timelines: what "long" really looks like

Set expectations honestly so you don't mistake normal slowness for failure. A clean case (clear attendance proof, correct UPCs) typically resolves in weeks; tangled ones (swapped sheets, centre disputes) can run into months — one case I followed took three months end to end. The marksheet correction, once approved, reflects in the updated result and the ER tag lifts with it. During the wait, keep studying that paper lightly alongside current ones (the ER clearing plan's 80–20 split applies perfectly) so a worst-case reappear is a revision exercise, not a fresh disaster. And keep every receipt, every stamped copy, every name-and-date note — if the case ever needs escalation, your folder is your voice.

Folder secret: Maintain one physical folder: marksheet printout, admit card copy, fee receipt, attendance-sheet proof, application copy with receiving stamp, and a one-page log of every visit (date, office, person met, what was said). The day someone asks "where is your proof?", you slide the folder across. Cases with folders move; cases with memories wait.

The 4 mistakes that kill genuine cases

  • Waiting quietly after applying. The number-one case-killer. No follow-up for months = file asleep in a pile. Fortnightly visits are the treatment, not rudeness.
  • Only calling / only messaging. Calls leave no record and reach whoever picks up. Written applications with receiving stamps + in-person follow-ups move files; everything else is hope.
  • Skipping the attendance-sheet proof. Arguing "but I was there!" without the signed sheet is "you said versus record" — and record wins. Get that page first, argue second.
  • Ignoring the reappear window. Pride ("why should I refill, I already wrote it!") versus prudence. Filing the backup form doesn't weaken your correction case — it insures your semester.

For the academic side of this mess, read the ER rules post (what an uncorrected Absent becomes) and the SGPA decoder (how the corrected grade flows back into your numbers).

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: How long does correction actually take?
Weeks for clean cases, months for tangled ones — three months in the case I watched closest. Start day one, follow up fortnightly, and judge progress by movement (acknowledgments, forwards), not just the final marksheet.

Q2: Will this affect my job / PG applications meanwhile?
Carry the folder: application copy with stamp + attendance proof + a short factual note. Most verifiers accept documented pending-correction; what blocks you is an unexplained ER with no paper trail. Build the trail from week one.

Q3: Should I really fill the reappear form while fighting the case?
Yes, if the window opens before resolution. It's insurance, not admission of absence — if the correction lands, the backup lapses unused. A missed window, by contrast, is irrecoverable time.

Q4: Regular vs distance — is one route harder?
Different desks, same difficulty class. Regular runs college exam office → examination branch; distance runs learner-support office + dashboard channels + visits. Both cases I saw got fixed. The variable is persistence, not mode.

Q5: Invigilator error caused it (wrong sheet) — does that change anything?
It strengthens your case (witnesses + register vs sheet mismatch) but doesn't shortcut it — same six steps, same folder. Note the invigilator's name and room in your application while memories are fresh.

Last-minute advice from the author

An "Absent" you didn't earn feels personal. It isn't — it's a data error with a documented fix. Start the six steps today, not after the shock fades, and keep the folder current. The system corrects genuine cases; it just makes you walk the corridor to prove it.
