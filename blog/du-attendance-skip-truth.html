Can You Skip GE, SEC, VAC Classes? Honest Math

I ran 8% attendance in Sem 1 and paid for it in marks and panic. Here's exactly which bunks cost what.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the question every DU fresher asks by October — and the answer seniors get wrong in both directions.

In Sem 1, I treated attendance like a suggestion. Fresh freedom, a 1-hour-45-minute commute each way, society auditions every evening — college felt optional, so I opted out. End of semester: roughly 8–10% overall. The damage wasn't one thing, it was three: I lost attendance marks in every paper, two VAC/SEC teachers graded my files colder because they'd never seen my face, and in November the department notice board carried a detention-warning list with my name on it. I spent the last month attending every single class like a hostage, clawing back to the safe line. Never again — but also never the opposite extreme, because perfect attendance at the cost of all societies and sleep is its own failure mode.

Here's the rub: the forums are full of two confident lies. Lie one: "attendance doesn't matter, I got A+ with zero classes." Lie two: "attend everything or you're finished." The truth is a spreadsheet: attendance converts to marks on a slab, counts toward a threshold, and varies by college strictness. Once you see the actual numbers, bunking becomes a budgeted decision instead of a gamble. (Distance learners: you don't have daily attendance — your equivalent is assignment deadlines, covered in the internals system. This post is for those with classrooms to skip.)

From three years of watching attendance strategies succeed and explode — including my own 8% semester — here is the honest math: the threshold, the 5-mark slab, overall-vs-paper counting, the bunk-priority ranking, and the recovery plan if you're already in the red.

The hard truth: attendance is marks + a gate

Attendance does two separate jobs, and confusing them causes every bad decision. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • 5 marks per paper, on a slab. Every theory paper carries attendance weight inside internals — typically 5 marks graded by percentage bands (top band around 85%+ for full marks, sliding down through the 70s; confirm your college handbook's exact cutoffs). Five marks per paper across six papers is thirty marks of SGPA decided by presence. That's not pocket change — that's a grade boundary in two papers.
  • The ~67% line is a gate, not a reward. Most colleges require roughly two-thirds (67%) attendance overall to sit exams without trouble — below it come warning lists, undertakings, and in strict departments, detention from papers. The 5-mark slab rewards the top; the 67% line punishes the bottom. You must clear the gate first, then optimise the slab.
  • Overall counting helps you — unevenly. In most colleges attendance is counted overall, which means strong-attendance papers cushion weak ones. That's why "attend only Core and GE" roughly works: Core + GE carry the most classes, so they carry your percentage. But some departments and some teachers track paper-wise strictly — one viral "overall only" claim doesn't override your department's notice board.

The bunk-priority ranking (skip smart, not random)

The golden rule seniors actually follow: attend Core + GE always (they're 4 credits each — see the GE picker for why GE deserves respect), keep SEC/VAC/AEC above the safe line, and spend your limited bunk budget on low-weight slots. Two classes of a 2-credit paper skipped casually is a bigger dent than it feels — small denominators swing fast.

Before you bunk: the 3-question check

  • Is anything submittable/testable that day? Assignment due, class test, file check, presentation — missing these costs 10-mark chunks, never just attendance. Check the class group the night before, every time.
  • How strict is this teacher? Strictness varies teacher to teacher, not just college to college. The lenient-professor paper absorbs bunks; the strict one's paper doesn't. Calibrate per teacher in the first month — ask second-years, they know exactly.
  • Where's my overall % this month? Track monthly, not semesterly. Above 80%: you have budget. 70–80%: essentials only. Below 70%: full-attendance mode till you're safe — no societies, no trips, no "one last bunk".
Bunk-budget secret: Treat attendance like money — a monthly budget, not vibes. Roughly: one day off per month keeps you above 85% across a ~90-working-day semester; two days a month lands near 75%; three-plus and you're negotiating with the 67% gate. Fest season (Feb–Mar) eats bunks invisibly — pre-save budget before it, because society work never feels like skipping until the notice board says otherwise.

In the red? The recovery plan

Below 70% mid-semester is recoverable — below it with one month left is an emergency, but the protocol is the same. Full attendance from today: zero optional bunks, sit through the dull lectures, sign every sheet (verify your name is actually marked — proxies and missed sheets are the classic recovery-killers). Talk to the strict teachers directly and early: "sir/ma'am, I'm at X%, I'm attending everything now" — teachers help visible recoveries far more than invisible absences. And learn the lesson structurally: the commute, the sleep schedule, the society load — fix the cause (I moved my society work to weekends and cut my travel by shifting one tutorial), not just the symptom. My own climb from ~10% to 70%+ took one disciplined semester; the marksheet thanked me in every paper's internal column (full breakdown in the 25-mark file system).

The 4 attendance mistakes that cost the most

  • Believing "zero classes, A+" stories. Possible in one lenient paper, catastrophic as a system. Survivorship bias: nobody posts "I skipped everything and got detained". The notice board posts those names instead.
  • Skipping submission/test days. A bunked lecture costs a fraction of 5 marks; a bunked class test costs up to 10. Always check the class group before deciding — admin days outrank all bunk logic.
  • Fest-season blindness. Society weeks feel productive, not absent. They count exactly the same as sleeping in. Pre-save bunk budget before fest season, and cap society weekdays in writing with yourself.
  • Never verifying the sheet. Present-but-unmarked happens — proxies, circulated sheets that skip your bench, name mismatches. Glance at the sheet when it passes; one unverified month can erase a semester of discipline.

For the marks these decisions protect, read the 25-mark file system and the 9+ CGPA playbook — attendance is the first column of both.

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: Is attendance counted overall or per subject?
Overall in most colleges — which is why Core + GE attendance carries your percentage. But departments vary and strict teachers track their own papers, so treat your department's notice board as the final authority, not a forum comment.

Q2: Can I skip all SEC/VAC/AEC classes then?
No — that's the most expensive version of this advice. Small denominators swing fast: a few bunks crater a 2-credit paper's percentage, and file-grading teachers remember absent faces. Attend them lightly but consistently, and never miss their submission days.

Q3: What actually happens below 67%?
Warning lists first, then undertakings/parent calls, and in strict departments, detention from papers — meaning you don't sit the exam at all. The exact chain varies by college, which is why "my friend was fine at 40%" is not data. Stay above the line; it's cheaper than finding out.

Q4: Medical emergency / long commute — any relief?
Colleges have medical-leave and condonation procedures, but they run on documents submitted on time — certificates filed months later rarely help. If your commute is brutal, front-load attendance early in the semester while energy is high; don't plan to "recover later".

Q5: Societies vs attendance — how do toppers balance?
Budget, not sacrifice: one bunk-day a month baseline, essentials-only during tests, full-attendance mode below 70%. Toppers in societies aren't attending less — they're skipping only low-weight slots and never admin days.

Last-minute advice from the author

Attendance is the only part of your SGPA decided by showing up. Check your percentage this week — not next month — and set the monthly budget today. Future-you, sitting exams without a detention scare and banking full slab marks, says thanks.
