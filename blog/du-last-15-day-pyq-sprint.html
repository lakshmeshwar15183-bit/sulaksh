DU Last 15 Days: The PYQ Sprint Plan

Theory exams from December 4. I wasted my first Sem's last fortnight on new chapters — here's the sprint that replaced it.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the fortnight that decides semesters — the last 15 days.

In my first Sem, I spent the final two weeks doing exactly the wrong thing: opening new chapters. Everyday a new unit, every night a new panic, zero papers solved. I walked into the first exam having "covered" everything and practised nothing — my hand cramped on page 9, I left a 10-marker half-done, and time management (not knowledge) cost me a grade. Next sem I flipped it: no new topics after day 15, only PYQs, timed papers, and one-page sheets. Same brain, same syllabus. SGPA jumped 1.1 points. The syllabus didn't change — the fortnight did.

Here's the rub: with DU odd-sem theory exams beginning December 4, 2026, the last 15 days are not for learning — they're for converting. Everything you know must become writable-in-3-hours, and everything you don't know must be strategically abandoned. Students who grasp this outscore harder-working peers every single cycle.

From running this sprint three times now (mine + juniors I coach), here is the day-by-day plan: what to solve, what to skip, how to sleep, and how to use the 15-minute reading time like a topper.

The hard truth about the last 15 days

More hours in the last fortnight don't help — more papers do. Your rank in 15 days is decided by output, not input. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • No new topics after today. A new unit started now eats 2 days and yields one shaky answer. The same 2 days on PYQs polish four strong answers. New material now manufactures panic, not marks — freeze the syllabus as it stands.
  • Timed papers beat re-reading, 10-to-1. Reading notes feels productive and tests nothing. One 3-hour timed paper exposes hand speed, structure gaps, and weak units simultaneously. Every alternate day gets a timed sitting in this plan — non-negotiable.
  • Sleep is a study technique. A rested brain recalls frameworks; a 3-am brain drops them. The toppers sleeping 7 hours aren't lazy — they're protecting recall. All-nighters in the last week cost more than they buy, every time.

Pull all papers from Sulaksh PYQs matched to your UPCs, and check dates against the tentative datesheet + odd-sem 2026 guide so the paper order in your sprint matches the real exam order — always revise the first exam's subject in the final 3 days, not the last one.

Days 15–11: Map + first blood. Sit one full past paper per subject under 3-hour exam conditions — phone off, desk clear. Grade yourself brutally against the 10-year answers: which units bled? Those weak units are your only syllabus now. Start one-page sheets per unit alongside: one quote/diagram/formula per unit, nothing more. This is the 3-pass method compressed — map (Pass 1) and solve (Pass 2) in five days.

  • Step 1: Timed paper → circle every answer below 60% quality.
  • Step 2: Those circled units become the fix-list. Everything else is maintenance.
  • Step 3: Sheets begun for every unit — even strong ones. The night before the exam, sheets are all you'll read.

Days 10–4: Fix the leaks. All study time goes to fix-list units: frameworks memorised (4–5 headings per answer), one diagram per unit drawn from memory twice, then a second timed paper per subject to confirm the leak closed. Strong units get 15-minute sheet reviews only — no full re-study. If a unit still won't stick after two attacks, abandon it strategically: one sacrificed 10-marker beats three half-prepared papers.

Days 3–1: Sheets only + sleep. No new topics. No full papers. Read one-page sheets on rotation, redraw each diagram once, recite each framework aloud once. Evenings end by 11 — the exam hall runs on recall, and recall runs on sleep. Pack the admit folder now (see admit-card fixes + download guide): printed card ×2, IDs, fee receipt.

Sprint secret I learnt late: The second timed paper per subject is where grades jump — the first paper finds leaks, the second proves fixes. Students who solve one paper improve a little; students who solve two improve a lot. And the night-before rule is absolute: sheets + sleep beat any new chapter, no matter how scary that unprepared unit looks.

What to skip (the abandon list)

  • New chapters. Frozen from today. If it wasn't started by day 15, it doesn't exist.
  • Senior's CBCS-era notes. Old-pattern units waste revision slots — verify every unit against your current UGCF syllabus first.
  • All-nighters. One all-nighter costs two days of recall. Sleep 7 hours; the sheets will still be there at 6 am.
  • New guides and new YouTube series. Same content, new packaging, zero additional marks. Your 10-year + sheets are enough.

For the engine underneath this sprint, read the 3-pass PYQ method in full; for the longer runway version, the 30-day plan. Same system, three timelines.

Exam-day: the 15-minute reading time strategy

  • Minutes 0–5: rank every question. Mark each as ✅ (strong), ⚠️ (attemptable), ❌ (weakest). Attempt order: strong first, attemptable second, weakest last — never question-paper order.
  • Minutes 5–10: skeleton the two hardest attempts. Jot 4 headings + 1 diagram idea per weak answer in the margin. Skeletons turn panic-blanks into step-marked pages.
  • Minutes 10–15: time-box the paper. 30–35 minutes per long answer, last 15 minutes buffer. Write the per-question deadline on the paper — then obey it like a train timetable.
  • Attempt everything. A structured 2-page attempt at the weakest question beats a blank by 4–5 grace marks. Blanks are the only unforgivable answer.

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: Syllabus unfinished with 15 days left — what now?
Freeze it. VIP-filter the remaining units via 5-year PYQs, keep the repeating core, abandon the rest strategically. A frozen 70% prepared to 90% quality beats a sprawling 100% prepared to 40%.

Q2: How many papers should I solve per subject?
Two timed papers minimum — first finds leaks, second confirms fixes. Three if time allows; beyond that, returns diminish. Quality of review beats quantity of sittings.

Q3: All-nighter before the exam — yes or no?
No. Sleep 7 hours, revise sheets till 10–11 pm, wake early for one final sheet pass. All-nighters trade guaranteed recall for anxious re-reading — a bad exchange at 3 am.

Q4: New topic appeared in the datesheet-gap — start it?
Only if it's a VIP-repeating unit (check 5-year frequency). Otherwise no — gaps between papers are for sheets + rest + the next paper's frameworks, not fresh chapters.

Q5: What if my centre is far / admit card has issues?
Handle admin in the Days 15–11 window, not the final week: verify the card now (fixes here), visit the centre early, pack the folder. Admin shocks in the last 3 days destroy revision rhythm.

Last-minute advice from the author

Fifteen days feels short until you count what fits: 7+ timed papers, 20+ one-page sheets, and 7-hour sleeps — that's a full semester conversion, not a compromise. Freeze the syllabus today, solve the first paper tomorrow morning, and let the sprint carry you to December 4. Go finish it.
