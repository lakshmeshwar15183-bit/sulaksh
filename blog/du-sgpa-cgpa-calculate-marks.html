DU SGPA/CGPA: Decode Your Marksheet (With Example)

Result day chaos — "8.2 SGPA, so percentage kitni?" My floor did this maths wrong for a year. Here's the exact formula.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the maths everyone gets wrong on result day.

Last year our floor celebrated an 8.2 SGPA like it was 82% — and mourned a 7.4 like it was failure. Both reactions were wrong, because nobody actually knew the formula. One senior claimed "CGPA into 10", another said divisions don't exist anymore, a third insisted internals don't count in SGPA. Three confident people, three wrong answers, one very confused floor. So I sat down with two actual marksheets, a calculator, and the university's grading scheme — and worked it out line by line. This post is that evening, written down so your floor doesn't repeat ours.

Here's the rub: DU's 10-point system looks simple (a number out of 10) but hides three different things — SGPA (this semester), CGPA (whole degree so far), and the percentage/division recruiters ask for. Mix them up and you'll either panic over nothing or relax over something real.

From decoding marksheets with juniors for three years now, here is the exact formula, a worked example with real numbers, and what edge cases (ERs, improvements) do to the maths.

The hard truth about DU grades

Your marks become grades, grades become points, and points get weighted by credits. Most confusion comes from skipping a step. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • Marks → grade → points is fixed. Every paper's marks convert to a letter grade (O, A+, A, B+...), each grade carries fixed grade points (10, 9, 8, 7...). Roughly: O = 90–100%, A+ = 80–89%, A = 70–79%, B+ = 60–69%, B = 55–59%, C = 50–54%, P = 40–49%, F = below 40%. Your marksheet prints the grade; the points hide behind it.
  • Credits are weights, not decoration. SGPA = Σ (grade points × credits) ÷ Σ credits. A 4-credit DSC moves your SGPA twice as much as a 2-credit SEC/VAC/AEC. An A+ in a 4-credit paper beats an O in a 2-credit one — every time, mathematically.
  • SGPA ≠ CGPA ≠ percentage. SGPA covers one semester; CGPA averages all semesters so far (credit-weighted); percentage (CGPA × 9.5 on DU marksheets) is the recruiter-facing number. Three numbers, three uses — stop comparing them to each other.

Worked example: calculate an SGPA step by step

Take a typical Sem 1 with four papers. I'll use realistic grades — not a topper fantasy:

PaperCreditsGradePointsPoints × credits
DSC Core paper4A (Very Good)832
GE paper4A+ (Excellent)936
SEC paper2O (Outstanding)1020
VAC paper2B+ (Good)714

Add the last column: 32 + 36 + 20 + 14 = 102. Add the credits: 4 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 12. Divide: 102 ÷ 12 = 8.50 SGPA. Notice the lesson hiding inside: the O in the 2-credit SEC contributed 20, while the A in the 4-credit Core contributed 32. Credits decide. (This is also why the VAC playbook and GE picker matter — small papers and GE picks move this exact sum.)

CGPA: same formula stretched across semesters — total (points × credits) across all papers so far ÷ total credits so far. Sem 1 SGPA 8.50 on 12 credits + Sem 2 SGPA 7.90 on 12 credits → (102 + 94.8) ÷ 24 = 8.20 CGPA. One weaker semester dilutes; it doesn't destroy — consistency across semesters beats one spike.

Percentage: DU marksheets convert with CGPA × 9.5. So 8.20 CGPA → 77.9%. And divisions run on that number — broadly, 60%+ first division, 50–60% second division, below that pass (confirm your program handbook for exact cutoffs, since schemes vary). An 8.2 isn't "82%" and a 7.4 isn't failure — it's ~70%, a comfortable first division.

Calculator secret: To predict results, work backwards. Want 8.5 next sem on 12 credits? You need 102 point-credits. Lock the 2-credit papers first (20 + 18 = 38 from two strong SEC/VAC performances), leaving 64 from 8 Core/GE credits — that's an 8.0 average on the big papers, not perfection. Bank the small papers (see the 25-mark file system) and the big ones get easier.

What ERs and improvements do to the maths

  • An ER contributes zero — temporarily. Until cleared, the failed paper adds 0 point-credits while its credits still count, so that semester's SGPA sinks. After clearing (see the ER clearing plan), the fresh grade replaces it for CGPA. One cleared ER moves a full-degree CGPA by ~0.1 — visible, not fatal.
  • Improvements follow separate rules. Re-attempting a passed paper to raise marks runs under improvement provisions, which differ by program — check your college office rather than assuming ER-repeat forms apply.
  • Never sacrifice current papers for backlogs. An 80–20 split (current vs backlog) protects the bigger credit pool. Manufacturing two new ERs to avenge one old ER is the worst trade in this system.
  • Internals are inside the grades. The 25 internal marks per paper feed the paper's total marks → grade → points. Banked internals (see the file system) quietly raise every grade in this calculation.

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: SGPA vs CGPA — what's the actual difference?
SGPA averages one semester's papers (credit-weighted); CGPA averages all semesters so far the same way. Recruiters and PG forms ask CGPA (final) or percentage — SGPA is your semester report card.

Q2: Is CGPA × 9.5 the official conversion?
Yes — DU marksheets carry the CGPA × 9.5 equivalent. So 8.20 → 77.9%. Don't use ×10, and don't compare CGPA digits directly with percentages.

Q3: What CGPA gives a first division?
Broadly, 60%+ (≈6.32 CGPA) is first division and 75%+ (≈7.9 CGPA) first division with distinction — but confirm your program handbook, since exact cutoffs and distinction rules vary by scheme.

Q4: How badly does one ER hurt?
That semester's SGPA dips hard (zero points for the paper), but post-clearing CGPA impact is ~0.1 for a single ER across a degree. Clear it in the very next cycle and move on.

Q5: Do 2-credit papers really matter in this maths?
Enormously per hour. In the example above, the SEC O contributed 20 of 102 point-credits — a fifth of the SGPA from one compact paper. That's why small papers are SGPA boosters, not distractions.

Save this page, run your own numbers with your grades, and argue with seniors using arithmetic instead of vibes.
