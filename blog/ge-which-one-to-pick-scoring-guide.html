Which GE to Pick in DU? A Scoring Guide

4 credits — same weight as your Core. I picked mine by following friends in Sem 1. Don't be me.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the decision I got wrong in Sem 1 and paid for all year.

When GE options opened, my whole friend circle picked Economics. "Scope hai, bro." I was a History student who hadn't touched a demand curve since Class 10. I spent the semester drowning in diagrams I didn't understand while my actual strength — theory papers with case-based answers — sat unused. I scraped a B. My roommate, also History, picked Indian Constitution as her GE, overlapped half of it with her PolSci-adjacent Core readings, quoted Article numbers like a topper, and walked out with an O. Same effort. Two grades apart. The only difference was the pick.

Here's the rub: GE is 4 credits — it moves your SGPA exactly as much as a Core paper. Yet students pick it in ten minutes based on friends, rumours, or "scope". Regular-college students realise the mistake by mid-sem when the syllabus feels alien; SOL students realise it when the guide book reads like a foreign language. And by then the option form is locked.

From navigating this university business for three years now, I can tell you — there is no "best GE", only the best GE for you. Three filters decide it: overlap with what you already study, marking style that suits you, and PYQ availability. Let's get into the exact method.

The hard truth about GEs

Picking a GE for "scope" or friends is how toppers manufacture their only B. The paper doesn't care about scope — it cares whether you can write what it asks for 3 hours. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • Overlap beats interest. A PolSci-adjacent student taking Indian Constitution reuses half her readings. An Eco student taking Indian Economy reuses data and diagrams. Overlap means every hour counts twice — once for GE, once for Core. Interest without overlap means double syllabus.
  • Marking style must suit you. Theory-strong? Constitution, History, and literature GEs reward structured essays with cases. Number-comfortable? Economy and commerce GEs reward tables and diagrams. Pick the game you can win: a brilliant essayist in a numerical GE is a fish climbing a tree.
  • No PYQs = no pick. If you can't find 3 years of PYQs for that GE-paper-code combination, you're gambling blind. DU repeats 60–70% from the same core every 2–3 years — but only if you can see the pattern first.

The 5-step picking method (do this before the form locks)

Steps 1–2: Shortlist by overlap, not friends. List your Core subjects and adjacent readings. Circle every GE sharing a unit with them — that's your overlap shortlist of 2–3. Strike off anything your friends picked that isn't on this list. Their overlap is not your overlap.

  • Step 1: Write your Core papers + the topics you already enjoy writing about.
  • Step 2: Match GEs to that list. Two overlaps minimum to stay shortlisted.
  • Step 3: Pull 3 years of PYQs per shortlisted GE (match the UPC — codes differ across semesters, and Kamla Nagar guides mix them up).

Steps 4–5: The writability test + medium check. Read one full PYQ per GE and ask: "Can I write 3 pages on 4 of these 6 questions today, with a week of prep?" If yes, it stays. Then confirm the question paper comes in your medium (Hindi/English) and check the class timing doesn't clash with your Core — a scoring GE you can't attend is a B with extra steps. File your option form only after both checks.

Picking secret I learnt late: Toppers don't pick the "easiest" GE — they pick the GE whose PYQs look most writable to them. Ten minutes with 3 past papers predicts your grade better than a month of senior advice. And never pick a GE with zero available PYQs — 40% pass with no pattern visibility is a gamble, not a plan.

The 3 picking mistakes that cost SGPA

  • Following friends. Their Core overlap is not yours. A GE that's revision for them is fresh syllabus for you.
  • Chasing "scope". Scope doesn't write answers. A "high-scope" GE you hate is a B; a boring-overlap GE is an O.
  • Ignoring medium + PYQs. Picking a GE whose paper isn't comfortably available in your medium, or with no PYQs to verify the pattern — gambling with 4 credits.
  • Forgetting it's 4 credits. GE moves SGPA as much as Core. Budget study time accordingly — never "wing" it in the last 3 days.

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: Which GE is the easiest for a non-Eco, non-PolSci student?
There is no universal easiest — but the writability test decides in 10 minutes. Pull 3 PYQs each for Constitution and Economy, try outlining answers, and pick the one where you fill pages faster. Most humanities students find Constitution's case-based theory more writable; number-comfortable students prefer Economy's data tables.

Q2: Should I pick the same GE as my friends for combined study?
Only if it also passes your overlap + writability test. Combined study helps for a week; a mismatched GE hurts for the whole semester. Study groups can share PYQ methods across different GEs anyway.

Q3: Can I change my GE after the form locks?
Usually not without a painful admin process — most colleges allow corrections only in a short window. Treat the option form as final: do the PYQ check before submitting, not after.

Q4: Does GE really affect SGPA as much as Core?
Yes — SGPA weights by credits, and GE is 4 credits like DSC. An O in GE lifts you exactly as much as an O in Core. That's why this pick deserves an evening of research, not ten minutes.

Q5: What if my college offers very few GE options?
Then run the same method on the short menu: overlap first, PYQ writability second. Fewer options just means a faster decision — and check Sulaksh PYQs for whichever codes your college actually offers.

Last-minute advice from the author

I know the GE form looks like a formality next to admission chaos. It isn't — it's a 4-credit decision. Spend one evening on the writability test before you submit, and you'll thank yourself every result day for three years.
