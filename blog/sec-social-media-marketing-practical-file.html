SEC Social Media Marketing: Practical File + 30-Mark Pattern

2 credits, fully practical — your file decides your grade before you enter the hall. I fumbled mine in Sem 2; here's the exact fix.

← All Guides

By Sulaksh Editorial · Reviewed by Sulaksh Editorial Team · Updated 30 September 2026 · 8 min read

Hey all! I'm back with the one that saved my Sem 2 SGPA after Sem 1 humbled me.

In Sem 2 I took SEC Social Media Marketing thinking "phone to main din bhar chalata hoon, kitna hard hoga?" Then the teacher asked for my practical file in week 6 — a full marketing plan with a content calendar, mock ad creatives, and engagement metrics — and I had three screenshots and vibes. I spent two panicked nights in the library building the file from scratch while my DSC notes sat untouched. Never again.

Here's the rub: almost every DU student picks this SEC because it sounds fun, then discovers it's a file-subject. Regular-college students juggle it with DSCs, SOL students get almost no practical guidance, and everyone leaves the file for the last week. Then internals get announced and you realise 25 marks were decided by a file you haven't started.

From navigating this university business for three years now, I can tell you — this is the easiest A/O in your semester if your file is right. Teachers check files fast and in bulk; a neat, complete file with a real plan gets full internals almost by default. Let's get into the exact roadmap.

The hard truth about this SEC

Studying Social Media Marketing like a theory paper will fail you. The syllabus reads light — platforms, content, marketing plans, analytics — but you can't mug your way through a practical file in one night. Three frameworks to keep in mind:

  • Questions repeat every 2–3 years. DU reuses the same core: marketing-plan format, content-calendar design, reach vs engagement vs conversion, one case study (usually a small business going online). Do last 5 years' papers and you've covered 60–70% of what's coming.
  • 40% is pass, but the file is the safe zone. Regular/NCWEB colleges usually run this as 30-mark theory (3 questions × 10 marks, 1 hour) + practical/file internals; SOL runs 60-mark theory (6 × 10, 2 hours). Confirm your college handout — but either way, a complete file locks your internals before theory even starts.
  • Screenshots beat paragraphs. A one-page mock creative with captions and hashtags gets more marks than three pages of "social media is important because...". Examiners reward doing, not defining.

Paper pattern and marking (what to actually expect)

SectionTypical askHow to answer
Unit 1 — PlatformsInstagram vs YouTube vs LinkedIn for a business (10 marks)Comparison table + one-line verdict per platform
Unit 2 — Content7-day content calendar + 2 mock creatives (10–15 marks)Actual calendar grid + captions with hashtags
Unit 3 — Plan & metricsFull marketing plan + reach/engagement/ROI reading (15–18 marks)Objective → audience → budget → calendar → metrics flow

Find your Unique Paper Code (UPC) on your fee receipt first — this SEC runs under slightly different codes across semesters. Match the UPC before picking a 10-year from Kamla Nagar market or a Shivdas/Satish guide. Wrong-code notes are the #1 reason students prepare the wrong units.

The 7-day practical blueprint

Days 1–2: Gather + filter (don't study yet). Get the official syllabus PDF, borrow a 10-year/SHIVDAS guide from your local market, and pull 5 years of PYQs from Sulaksh PYQs. Take one notebook and write the 10 most repeated questions per unit. Highlight them — these are your VIP questions. Everything else is backup.

  • Step 1: Official syllabus → mark the 3 units and their weightage.
  • Step 2: 10-year guide → tick which VIP questions have ready-made answers.
  • Step 3: Pick your file business — a real local shop, your friend's thrift page, anything with a real audience.

Days 3–5: Build the file (this IS the study). One section per day: Day 3 — platform comparison + why you picked Instagram/YouTube for your business. Day 4 — 7-day content calendar with actual post captions, timings, and hashtags. Day 5 — mock budget (even ₹2,000 split across 3 boosted posts) + metrics page defining reach, engagement rate, and conversion with one worked example. Bullet points only — the file writes your theory answers for you.

Days 6–7: Answer writing + mock. Most 10-mark questions need 3–4 pages minimum. Use this structure every time: Introduction (2-line definition) → Main body (headings + sub-headings) → Visual (calendar grid / metrics table / funnel diagram) → Conclusion (3 lines). Then take one past paper timed — Regular/NCWEB students practice 1-hour speed (20 min per answer), SOL students full 2-hour sittings. Your hand needs the muscle memory.

Marking secret I learnt late: A file with a dated content calendar + 2 mock creatives + one metrics table gets full internals in most colleges even if your theory is average. And in theory, drawing the marketing funnel (awareness → consideration → conversion) with one example is worth 3 marks by itself. Never leave it out.

Pro tips for exam day (small things, extra marks)

  • Attempt everything. Blank = zero. A 2-page structured attempt at a half-known question fetches 4–5 grace marks via step-marking.
  • Time-box ruthlessly. Regular/NCWEB: 20 minutes per 10-mark answer in your 1 hour. SOL: 20 minutes per answer across 2 hours — don't polish Q1 and abandon Q6.
  • Verify admit card the night before. Centre address, reporting time, and your photo on exam.du.ac.in — last-minute panic kills recall.
  • Submit the file early. A file submitted on time with an index page and page numbers looks like full marks before the teacher reads a word.

Frequently asked questions (answered by the author)

Q1: Is Social Media Marketing really scoring, or should I focus on Core instead?
Both — but per hour, this SEC gives more SGPA. 2 credits with a file-based pattern means one week of building the file can lift you from a B to an O. Core still decides more weight, so do Core first, SEC second, never SEC zero.

Q2: Shivdas, Satish, or 10-year — which one for this SEC?
10-year solved papers first — you see the exact repeat pattern (plan format, calendar, metrics). Shivdas is best for quick point-wise revision in the last 3 days. Match the UPC, else you'll prepare the wrong units.

Q3: How many pages for a 10-mark plan question?
3–4 pages: objective + audience (1 page) + calendar/metrics table (1–2 pages) + budget + conclusion. Grids and funnels, not one long paragraph block.

Q4: What if I get an ER in SEC?
You get an 'ER' (Essential Repeat) on the marksheet for that paper only — no year loss. File the repeat form next cycle and clear just that paper. But an ER still hits your SGPA that sem, so finish the file now.

Q5: Do file marks save me if theory is weak?
Internals are mandatory and counted, but you must still cross 40% in theory separately. A 24/25 file with a near-blank theory sheet is still an ER. Pass theory on its own.

Last-minute advice from the author

I know the SEC guilt — you picked the "fun" paper, ignored the file for two months, and now internals are on the notice board. Forget that. Pick your business today, build the calendar tonight, and write one funnel answer tomorrow. Past papers are gold here — use them wisely.
