The 3-Pass PYQ Method

Previous year papers are the highest-information material you own. Here is the three-pass system that converts them into a prediction tool, practice set and revision filter.

← All Guides

Ask any serious aspirant about previous year questions and they will nod wisely. Then watch what they actually do: download fifteen PDFs, scroll through them once, and go back to their notes. PYQs are the highest-information study material you own, and this is the system that turns them into marks.

Why PYQs matter more than any prediction video

Every exam — DU semesters, SSC, banking, UPSC prelims — has patterns. Not the questions themselves, but the shape of questions: which units get repeated, how marks are distributed between theory and application, whether options in MCQs are designed to trap specific mistakes. A prediction video guesses. Three years of papers prove.

Pass 1: The map (before you start studying)

Do this at the very start of preparation:

  • Take the last 3–5 years of papers and your official syllabus side by side.
  • For every question, mark its unit on the syllabus copy.
  • Count. Now rank units by frequency.

This produces your personal priority list. Units with constant appearances get first reading; units that appeared once in five years get skimmed or skipped if time is short. Most students discover 30–50% of their syllabus barely gets examined.

Pass 2: The rehearsal (during preparation)

As you finish each unit, immediately attempt that unit's questions from past papers — without notes first, then verify.

  • Written exams: write full answers for at least two questions per unit, timed. Reading a solution feels like knowing; writing reveals the truth.
  • MCQ exams: attempt in blocks of 25–50 questions. Log every wrong answer in an "error notebook" with one line on why you got it wrong — concept gap, silly mistake, or misread question.

The error notebook becomes your most valuable document by the end. It is literally a list of your marks leaking out.

Pass 3: The simulation (last weeks)

In the final phase, solve full papers in one sitting, strictly timed — morning slot if your exam is in the morning. This trains three things no chapter can: stamina, time allocation across sections, and the decision-making of skipping a question instead of bleeding minutes on it.

Scoring rule: after each simulated paper, spend equal time reviewing it. An unreviewed mock is entertainment; a reviewed one is training.

Where to keep everything

Keep your PYQ collection organised by subject and year in one place rather than scattered across chats and downloads folders. On Sulaksh every subject folder already separates 📄 PYQs from notes and syllabus, so the Pass 1 mapping takes minutes, not evenings.

Three passes: map it, rehearse it, simulate it. That's the entire method — and it converts a pile of old PDFs into a scoring system.

FAQ

How many years of PYQs should I solve? Three years minimum for pattern mapping, five if the exam format changed recently. Older papers still help for practice volume after the mapping pass.

Solving PYQs before studying feels useless since I know nothing. True? Pass 1 is not about solving — it is about counting which units get asked. You are building a map, not testing yourself.

Do PYQs repeat verbatim? Rarely word-for-word, but concepts and question shapes repeat constantly. That is precisely why pattern recognition beats memorising old answers.
