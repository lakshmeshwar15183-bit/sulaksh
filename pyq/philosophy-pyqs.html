Study Guide — Philosophy Honours (DU)

Philosophy Honours trains you to argue, not memorise. Examiners reward clear argument structure, accurate use of technical terms, and the ability to compare positions (Plato vs. Aristotle, Kant vs. Hume, Advaita vs. Dvaita).

Exam pattern & marking

8-semester programme under UGCF 2022. Early semesters cover Indian and Western philosophy (Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Plato, Aristotle, Descartes, Hume). Later semesters add logic, ethics, epistemology, and contemporary philosophy. Papers are typically 75 + 25 or 90 + 10.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Indian philosophy — Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Vedanta, Buddhism, Jainism.
  • Western philosophy — Plato, Aristotle, Descartes, Hume, Kant.
  • Logic — propositional logic, syllogism, fallacies.
  • Ethics — virtue ethics, deontology, utilitarianism.
  • Epistemology — perception, inference, testimony.

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • Describing a philosopher without their argument.
  • Confusing Indian and Western positions.
  • No examples or thought experiments.
  • Poor structure — no thesis or conclusion.

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Build concept maps per philosopher (position / argument / critique).
  2. Days 6–10: Revise logic (truth tables, syllogisms, fallacies).
  3. Days 11–15: Map PYQs to topics; note repeating questions.
  4. Days 16–20: Write 15-mark answers with thesis + argument + counter + conclusion.
  5. Days 21–25: Practice comparison questions (X vs. Y).
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers; fix time management.
