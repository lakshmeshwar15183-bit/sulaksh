B.Com (Hons) at Delhi University under UGCF 2022 is a 4-credit DSC per semester across 8 semesters, progressing from foundations to specialisation. Sem 1 covers Business Organisation, Financial Accounting and Business Laws; Sem 2-4 add Corporate Accounting, Cost Accounting, Business Statistics and Financial Management; Sem 5-6 shift to Auditing, GST and Financial Markets; and Sem 8 offers electives like DSC-8.1 The Economy of Bharat or DSC-8.2 Financial Technology and Analytics (each 4 credits, 3L+1T). DSC-8.1, for example, spans growth and development with Kautilya's Arthashastra, HDI and sustainability, Planning to NITI Aayog, agriculture/industry (four revolutions, MSMEs, disinvestment) and services/external sector (Make in India, BOP, Viksit Bharat), while earlier semesters focus on ledger, costing and law provisions. Exams are typically 75+25 or 90+10 with Section A compulsory and Section B attempt 4/6, where 50% of Sem 1-4 marks are step-based numericals. Examiners award marks for working notes even with a wrong final answer, and for theory using definition + provision + example (e.g., Section 2(j) Contract Act + case). A statistical profile of BOP or disinvestment with a table fetches more than textbook definitions — verify your paper code and the GE exclusion rule (ECON030/059 students must take FinTech) from your college before the exam.

Study Guide — B.Com (Hons) (DU)

B.Com (Hons) is accounting, law, and finance driven. Examiners reward correct journal entries, working notes, and provision-based answers. A correct working note fetches 8/12 even if the final figure is off.

Exam pattern & marking

8-semester programme under UGCF 2022. Sem 1: Business Organisation, Financial Accounting, Business Laws. Sem 2–4: Corporate Accounting, Cost Accounting, Business Statistics, Financial Management. Sem 5–6: Auditing, GST, Financial Markets. Sem 8: electives. Papers are typically 75 + 25 or 90 + 10.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Financial Accounting — journal, ledger, trial balance, final accounts.
  • Corporate Accounting — share issue, debentures, amalgamation.
  • Cost Accounting — marginal costing, absorption costing, variance analysis.
  • Business Laws — Contract Act, Sale of Goods, Companies Act.
  • GST & Auditing — input credit, audit procedures, vouching.

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • Wrong journal entries (debit/credit swapped).
  • Missing working notes.
  • Not showing steps in numericals.
  • Confusing provisions with sections in law answers.

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Make a working-notes sheet per Unit (accounting entries, formulas).
  2. Days 6–10: Solve 5 numericals per Unit under timed conditions.
  3. Days 11–15: Revise law provisions (Contract Act, Companies Act, GST).
  4. Days 16–20: Write 15-mark theory answers (definition + provision + example).
  5. Days 21–25: Practice high-weight Units (accounting, GST) using PYQ mapping.
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers; fix time management.
