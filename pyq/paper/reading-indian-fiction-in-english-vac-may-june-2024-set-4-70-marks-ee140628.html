Reading Indian Fiction in English is a 2-credit VAC (1-0-1) under UGCF 2022, taught in 2025-26 as a novel-reading and ethos paper (p.42 credit table). As per the official syllabus at du.ac.in/uploads/new-web/26102022_VAC.pdf, the course has Unit I How to Read a Novel (2 weeks) and Novel as Reflection of Society; Unit II (7 weeks) Bankim Chandra Chatterjee Anandamath (Sri Aurobindo/Barindra translation) with discussion points — patriotism/nationalism, Bande Mataram, history/romance, gender, natural calamity, genre/film; and Unit III (6 weeks) Chaman Nahal Azadi (Houghton Mifflin 1975) on partition, violence/trauma, autobiographical voices, history/politics/art and personal/political, plus 15 weeks practical (pp.42-43). The practical includes dramatic recitation, graphic plot outlining/storyboarding, classifying figurative language and character portrayal write-ups (p.43). Examination is 'Subject to directions from Examination Branch'. Students lose marks by retelling plot without the prescribed discussion points (e.g., Bande Mataram or trauma lens) and by skipping the figurative-language classification viva asks for (as of 12 Sept 2026).

Study Guide — English Honours (DU)

English Honours at Delhi University is a close-reading and form-aware programme. Examiners do not reward plot summaries; they reward accurate textual reference, a clear thesis in the first three lines, and awareness of form (sonnet vs. dramatic monologue vs. free verse). Treat every text as a small machine: what is said, how it is said, and one exact quote.

Exam pattern & marking

Most papers are 90 marks theory + 10 internal (or 75 + 25 depending on college). Expect one passage-based question (quote 1–2 lines), two 15-mark essays, and three 10-mark shorts. A 15-mark answer that opens with a definition, gives three points (concept + example + quote), one limitation, and a concluding “so what?” consistently crosses 11/15.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Chaucer’s General Prologue — character sketches, social satire, frame narrative.
  • Shakespearean drama — soliloquy, dramatic irony, tragic flaw.
  • Romantic poetry — nature, imagination, the sublime (Wordsworth, Keats, Shelley).
  • Post-colonial theory — hybridity, mimicry, subaltern (Bhabha, Spivak, Said).
  • Indian Writing in English — diaspora, identity, nation (Rushdie, Roy, Desai).

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing a plot summary instead of analysis.
  • Quoting from memory without checking the exact line.
  • Ignoring form — calling a sonnet “a poem” loses 2–3 marks.
  • Not linking the text to its historical/cultural context.

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Read the primary text; make a one-page sheet per text (what’s said / how it’s said / one quote).
  2. Days 6–10: Solve 2 past papers per semester under timed conditions.
  3. Days 11–15: Map every PYQ question to its Unit; note which Units repeat.
  4. Days 16–20: Write 15-mark answers using the 4-part template; get feedback.
  5. Days 21–25: Revise one-page sheets; memorise key quotes with line numbers.
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers; fix time management.

Verify final paper codes and unit lists from your college handout — this guide follows the broad UGCF/NEP outline.
