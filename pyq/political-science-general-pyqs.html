About This Collection — Political Science General

Political Science General at DU blends theory (liberty, justice, democracy) with Indian institutions and global politics. With 264 documents across 1 semesters, this collection orders syllabus and PYQs so you study concepts and their Indian application together.

Exam pattern: 90/100 marks with choices — e.g., “Answer 4 out of 6”. Theory papers ask “Compare Rawls with Nozick on justice” while India papers ask “Federalism — quasi or cooperative? Discuss with recent examples.” That pairing is deliberate.

Marking: A thesis + counter-thesis + Indian example fetch full marks. For theory, one quote (e.g., “Justice is the first virtue of social institutions” — Rawls) is enough; for Indian politics, one Supreme Court case or recent election data shows application.

How to use PYQs: Keep Unit 1 and Unit 4 of each paper open together — DU often pairs liberty with democracy, or federalism with representation. Make a one-page debate table per unit: left “concept”, right “critique + example”. That is exactly how verified notes here are formatted.

Study Guide — Political Science Honours (DU)

Political Science blends theory, Indian politics, and comparative/global politics. Examiners reward a clear thesis in the first three lines, a real-world example (Indian case or Supreme Court judgment), and a concluding “so what?”

Exam pattern & marking

4-credit DSC papers (3 lecture + 1 tutorial) across 8 semesters. Most papers are 75 + 25 or 90 + 10. Shorts are 10 marks, longs are 15. Examiners look for concept + critique + Indian example.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Political theory — liberty, equality, justice, Rawls/Nozick.
  • Indian constitution — federalism, rights, institutions, amendments.
  • Comparative politics — political systems, parties, elections.
  • Global politics — international relations, globalisation, security.
  • Public policy — policy cycles, implementation, evaluation.

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • No Indian example or case study.
  • Describing concepts without critique.
  • Poor structure — no thesis in the opening lines.
  • Confusing theory with current affairs.

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Build a debate table per Unit (concept / critique / Indian example).
  2. Days 6–10: Map PYQs to Units; note which Units repeat.
  3. Days 11–15: Write 15-mark answers with thesis + 3 points + conclusion.
  4. Days 16–20: Revise thinker quotes (Rawls, Ambedkar, Marx).
  5. Days 21–25: Practice short answers (10 marks, 200 words).
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers under timed conditions.
