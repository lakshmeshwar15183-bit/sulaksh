About This Collection — Economics

Economics at DU is mathematical and diagram-driven. This collection with 120 documents across 3 semester(s) separates Micro, Macro, Statistics and Maths so you revise the quantitative core first.

Exam pattern: 75 marks theory with numericals (40% weight) and theory (60%). Expect one 15-mark derivation (e.g., IS-LM, consumer equilibrium), two 10-mark numericals (elasticity, national income), and shorts on definitions. Without diagrams, scores plateau at 45/75.

Marking: Steps matter — writing the formula, substituting, and final unit (Rs., %). In theory, a labelled diagram (Indifference curves, AD-AS) with a 2-line explanation often gets 8/10 even with brief text.

How to use PYQs: For each unit, solve last 3 years’ numericals first, then theory. You will see 50% repeat — e.g., GDP vs GNP, multiplier, Phillips curve debate. Keep a formula sheet per semester; verified notes here do the same.

All papers here are UGCF/NEP, free to view. Cross-check the official syllabus for exact numerical weightage for your batch.

Study Guide — Economics Honours (DU)

Economics Honours is mathematical and diagram-driven. Examiners check formula, substitution, units, and labelled diagrams. One correct AD-AS or indifference-curve diagram often rescues 8/10.

Exam pattern & marking

Typically 75 marks theory + 25 internal (or 90 + 10). About 40% weight is numericals. Short answers are 10 marks, longs are 15. Steps carry half the marks.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Introductory Statistics — descriptive stats, probability, distributions.
  • Microeconomics — consumer equilibrium, elasticity, market structures.
  • Macroeconomics — national income, IS-LM, AD-AS, Phillips curve.
  • Mathematical Methods — matrices, calculus, optimisation.
  • Econometrics — OLS, hypothesis testing, regression.

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • Skipping steps in numericals.
  • Wrong units or missing labels on diagrams.
  • Confusing nominal vs. real values.
  • Not showing the formula before substitution.

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Build a formula sheet per Unit (stats, micro, macro, econometrics).
  2. Days 6–10: Make a diagram sheet (IS-LM, AD-AS, indifference curves).
  3. Days 11–15: Solve 10 numericals per Unit under timed conditions.
  4. Days 16–20: Write 15-mark theory answers with diagrams.
  5. Days 21–25: Revise high-weight Units (2–3) using PYQ mapping.
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers; check time management.
