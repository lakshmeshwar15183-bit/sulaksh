Business Organisation — BCH: GE-1.1 in the Commerce common pool as notified in 2022 and offered in 2025-26 — is a 4-credit generic elective (3 lectures +1 tutorial) that, in the verified CDN syllabus, runs five units of 12 hours each: Introduction (business as a system, ethics and social responsibility), Business Enterprises (sole proprietorship through MNC plus LLP, one-person company and mergers/takeovers), Business Environment (internal/external dimensions and SWOT/ETOP), Entrepreneurship (the entrepreneur-enterprise link and Skill India, Startup India, Make in India and glocalisation) and Contemporary Issues (learning organisations, workforce diversity, franchising, outsourcing, e-commerce, sustainability and digitalisation). A DU-specific assessment on page 2 is the practical: you are given a scenario — capital, liability and scale — and must choose a business form and justify it, plus a SWOT for a chosen firm.

About Business Management Organisation — Generic Elective at Delhi University

As a Generic Elective (GE) under DU's UGCF/NEP, Business Management Organisation carries 4 credits and lets you study outside your core discipline. Colleges offer it across Semesters 1-6. Fundamentals of business management. This page brings together syllabus, previous year papers and study material so you can see the taught order and exam pattern together.

What you will study (broad UGCF outline — verify from your college): The DU syllabus is organised into four units. For Business Management Organisation, the broad coverage is:

  • Unit 1: Management functions & theories
  • Unit 2: Planning, organising & staffing
  • Unit 3: Leadership, motivation & control
  • Unit 4: Business environment & case studies

Exam pattern & marking: Typically 4 credits, 75 marks theory + 25 internal/tutorial or 90+10 depending on college. Sections with choices — e.g., answer 4 out of 6. Units 2-3 carry the most weight, with diagram or data questions where applicable. Internal assessment includes tutorials and presentations. Examiners look for unit-wise structure, one relevant example per answer, and a clear conclusion.
