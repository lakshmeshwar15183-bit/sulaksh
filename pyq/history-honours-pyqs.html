About This Collection — History Honours

History Honours at DU (NEP/UGCF) is the most reading-intensive programme, spanning Ancient to Contemporary India and World History. This hub with 165 documents across 8 semesters groups every syllabus, PYQ and note chronologically, matching how DU teaches — not by random years.

Exam pattern: Typically 75 marks written + 25 internal. Expect one map-based question (10 marks, locate and explain 4 sites), two 15-mark essays (historiography + debate), and three 10-mark shorts. The trick is sources: DU asks “Reconstruct with epigraphy” or “Use archaeological vs literary sources” — answers without source discussion rarely cross 6/10.

Marking: Historiography name-drops help, but over-quoting without chronology hurts. Examiners check: clear period bracket, one primary source per answer, and a concluding “why this debate still matters” line.

How to use PYQs: Before reading, run the 3-pass method — first pass mark repeated units (e.g., Mauryan state, Bhakti movement, 1857), second pass write timed answers, third pass make a one-page timeline per unit. That compresses 300 pages into 20 revision pages.

Check the Learning Objectives and Suggested Readings on your syllabus — examiners lift questions verbatim from there. Always cross-check with your college.

Study Guide — History Honours (DU)

History at DU is chronology + source discussion + historiography. Examiners reward answers that name a period bracket, cite one primary source, and end with a concluding historiographical note — not generic narratives.

Exam pattern & marking

Typically 75 marks written + 25 internal. Expect one map-based question (locate and explain 4 sites), two 15-mark essays, and three 10-mark shorts. Answers without source discussion rarely cross 6/10.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Ancient India — sources (epigraphy vs. literary), Mauryan state, Gupta economy.
  • Medieval India — Sultanate administration, Mughal Mansabdari-Jagirdari, Rajput alliances.
  • Modern India — 1857, Bhakti movement, nationalism, Partition.
  • World history — historiography, colonialism, decolonisation.
  • Map work — 4 sites with one-line explanation each.

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • Writing without a clear period bracket.
  • No primary source (inscription, traveller account, or edict).
  • Over-quoting without chronology.
  • Ignoring the map question (free 10 marks).

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Build a timeline per Unit (Ancient → Medieval → Modern).
  2. Days 6–10: Make a map sheet — 4 sites per Unit with one-line notes.
  3. Days 11–15: Run the 3-pass PYQ method (mark units → timed answers → one-page sheet).
  4. Days 16–20: Write 15-mark essays with source + chronology + conclusion.
  5. Days 21–25: Revise historiography names (Thapar, Chandra, Singh).
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers under timed conditions.

Verify final unit details from your college handout.
