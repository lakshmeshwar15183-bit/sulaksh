Study Guide — Sanskrit Honours (DU)

Sanskrit Honours moves from kavya and prose to grammar, poetics, and Indian knowledge systems. Examiners reward accurate translation, grammatical analysis, and understanding of literary theory (alamkara, rasa, dhvani).

Exam pattern & marking

8-semester programme under UGCF 2022. Early semesters cover Classical Sanskrit Literature (Kavya), Critical Survey of Sanskrit Literature, and Vyakarana (grammar). Later semesters add poetics, philosophy, and Indian knowledge systems. Papers are typically 75 + 25 or 90 + 10.

High-yield units (from PYQ mapping)

  • Kavya — Meghadutam, Abhijnanashakuntalam, Niti literature.
  • Vyakarana — sandhi, samasa, karka, lakara.
  • Alamkara / Rasa — figures of speech, rasa theory, dhvani.
  • Darshana — Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Vedanta basics.
  • Sanskrit composition — translation, essay, letter.

Common mistakes that cost marks

  • Ignoring grammar (vyakarana) questions.
  • Weak translation without sandhi/samasa analysis.
  • Not linking kavya to alamkara theory.
  • Memorising without understanding rasa/dhvani.

30-day plan

  1. Days 1–5: Build grammar tables (sandhi, samasa, karka, lakara).
  2. Days 6–10: Read prescribed kavya; note key shlokas with meaning.
  3. Days 11–15: Map PYQs to texts and grammar topics.
  4. Days 16–20: Write translation + grammatical analysis answers.
  5. Days 21–25: Revise alamkara and rasa theory with examples.
  6. Days 26–30: Full mock papers; fix time management.
