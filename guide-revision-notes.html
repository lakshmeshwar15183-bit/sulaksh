Revision Notes That Actually Work

If your notes are a second textbook, you will never revise from them. The shrinking-note system: full notes → one-page sheets → a ten-point card.

← All Guides

Look at any student's notes folder before exams and you will find the same disease: 200-page "notes" that are just the textbook re-copied. When revision time comes, nobody revises from them — there is no time. Notes fail when they are a second textbook instead of a retrieval tool. The fix is shrinking notes.

The three-level system

  • Level 1 — Full notes (made during first reading): unit-wise, with definitions, examples and diagrams. Fine to be detailed; this is your reference shelf.
  • Level 2 — One page per topic (made during second reading): headings, sub-headings, key terms, one example each, plus the 2–3 PYQ questions asked from this topic. If it does not fit one side of A4, you have not compressed your thinking yet.
  • Level 3 — The 10-point card (exam week): per subject, ten bullet points covering only what you keep forgetting — tricky dates, formula exceptions, confusing pairs of concepts.

Revision then happens at the right altitude: Level 1 if months remain, Level 2 in the final month, Level 3 on exam morning.

Rules that make compression work

  • Write triggers, not sentences. "1857 → causes: doctrine, grease; events: Meerut→Delhi" is a revision note. A paragraph about 1857 is a textbook.
  • PYQs decide what enters Level 2. Topics never asked in five years get one line or none. Your one-pager is a betting sheet on the exam.
  • Handwrite Levels 2–3. Writing forces decisions; typing lets you hoard. The friction is the feature.
  • One colour rule for weak spots. Mark anything you failed to recall during practice in red/underline. Exam week, those marks ARE your syllabus.
Test tonight: pick yesterday's studied topic and write its Level-2 sheet from memory. Whatever you could not recall goes on the sheet first — you just built tomorrow's revision list.

Digital or paper?

Paper wins for Levels 2–3 because drawing arrows, boxes and memory-maps by hand engages recall better than scrolling. Digital is fine for Level 1 archives — searchable PDFs and organised folders (like subject-wise sections) save hunting time. Use both: digital library, paper revision sheets.

When to make each level

  1. Level 1: same day as learning, within 24 hours while memory is fresh.
  2. Level 2: after finishing the unit AND solving its past questions — never before, or you will guess wrong about what matters.
  3. Level 3: start 10 days before the exam; update daily from mock/practice errors.

Total revision material at exam time: a handful of one-pagers per subject and ten-point cards. That fits in one folder and one head — which is exactly the point.

FAQ

How many pages should Level-2 notes be? One side of A4 per topic. If content overflows, compress harder or split the topic honestly into two — overflow usually means you are copying instead of filtering.

Are digital notes acceptable? For archives yes, for revision sheets handwriting wins. The act of writing by hand forces selection decisions that typing lets you skip.

What if my exam is too near to make all three levels? Skip straight to Level-3 cards from past mistakes. Ten points per subject covering your weakest recalls is the fastest possible revision asset.
